# Design Playbook

The engine that keeps sites from converging. Build standards make every site work; this makes every site different.

## Why sites converge

Same instructions plus same model equals same site in different hex codes. Recoloring is not differentiation — a visitor reads **structure and type** before color. So variation has to happen at the structural layer.

Three mechanisms, every build:

1. **Menus, not defaults.** Hero, services layout, nav, and section rhythm are each chosen from a menu and logged. Nothing is a default.
2. **The build log.** `_system/RECIPE-LOG.md` records every build's choices so the studio can see its own patterns. It is documentation, not a constraint — repeating a prior client's choice is fine when it's what converts for the vertical.
3. **Asset-driven design.** The photo library dictates strategy, and no two clients have the same library. This is the strongest natural source of variation available.

## Asset-quality matrix — decide this first

Graded at Stage 0. Determines the entire design strategy. Not negotiable by taste.

| Grade | What it looks like | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | 50+ professional or well-shot images, consistent lighting, real people, varied subjects | **Photo-forward.** Full-bleed heroes, large image bands, minimal graphic intervention. Let the work be the design. Type stays quiet and confident. |
| **B** | 20–50 decent phone photos, inconsistent lighting and color | **Constrained and treated.** Tight crops, a unifying color grade or duotone, strong grid discipline, generous color blocks between image sections. Never place two inconsistent photos side by side at full size. |
| **C** | Under 20, poor lighting, watermarked, screenshots, mostly logo graphics | **Type-driven.** The design carries the site, not the photos. Big expressive typography, color blocks, texture, iconography or illustration, generous negative space. Use the two or three usable photos as small accents. Recommend a photo shoot in writing — highest-ROI thing the client can do. |

**Modifiers that override the grade**

- **No faces** → shift trust weight onto review quotes, credentials, and numbers. Design a first-class testimonial treatment, not an afterthought. Push hard for at least one owner photo.
- **Vehicle wrap exists** → derive the palette from the wrap. That's the brand customers actually see in the world, and it beats whatever hex codes the client typed into the form.
- **Before/after pairs exist** → a signature element waiting to happen. Detailing, painting, roofing, landscaping, and grooming all convert on transformation.
- **Video exists, even phone footage** → a short muted loop as hero background outperforms a static image in most trades, if stabilized and under 3MB.

## Vertical vibe library

Starting points. Each has an obvious read and a counter-read — both are legitimate; pick whichever fits the client's actual brand evidence.

**Plumbing / drain / rooter**
Obvious: urgency and trust. Saturated primary, bold condensed type, 24/7 badging, truck and uniform imagery, response-time promises.
Counter: family heritage — warm neutrals, slab serif, archival photos, "third generation."

**HVAC**
Obvious: comfort and climate — cool-to-warm logic, seasonal framing, efficiency numbers.
Counter: technical precision — mono numerics, spec-sheet layouts, engineering aesthetic.

**Roofing**
Obvious: sky and scale — drone photography, storm urgency, financing prominence, warranty length as hero stat.
Counter: craft and materials — close-up shingle, tile, metal texture; muted earth palette; material-sample grid.

**General contractor / construction**
Obvious: heavy grid, project portfolio as spine, concrete/black/amber, industrial type.
Counter: architectural restraint — near-white, thin type, enormous project photography, gallery sensibility.

**Handyman**
Obvious: approachable and organized — checklist aesthetic, friendly rounded type, clear service tiles.
Counter: personal brand — the owner *is* the business, portrait-led, first-person voice throughout.

**Windows / glass / doors**
Obvious: light and airy — whites, glass reflections, product galleries, energy data, warranty emphasis.
Counter: showroom luxury — dark editorial, dramatic lighting, product as furniture.

**In-home sales (water treatment, windows, solar)**
Obvious: consultative trust — appointment-first architecture, credential density, financing calculator, process explainer.
Counter: outcome-led — lead with the result in the home, lifestyle photography, soft sell.

**Painting**
Obvious: color as content — swatch systems, before/after, palette-driven design.
Counter: crisp minimal — the discipline of clean lines *is* the pitch. White space, precise rules, one accent.

**Mobile detailing**
Obvious: performance — dark base, high-gloss reflections, electric accent, motion, mono numerics, before/after slider.
Counter: concours luxury — cream or deep green, serif display, restrained, "we don't wash cars."

**Barbershop**
Rugged: dark, leather and wood texture, condensed uppercase, vintage badge lockups, warm amber.
Classic: navy and gold or black and cream, refined serif, thin rules, striped motifs, quiet luxury.
Urban: high contrast, streetwear influence, oversized type, saturated accent, editorial photography.

**Hair salon**
Chic: editorial, generous whitespace, muted palette, high-contrast serif, huge photography, sparse copy.
Warm/boho: terracotta and sage, organic shapes, textural, hand-drawn accents.
Bold/modern: saturated color blocking, geometric sans, graphic confidence.

**Pet grooming**
Obvious: bubbly — rounded type, saturated or pastel palette, illustration, pet photography, playful microcopy.
Counter: premium pet spa — muted sophistication, grooming as wellness rather than fun.

## Structural menus

Pick one from each, deliberately — nothing is a default. Log the choice in `_system/RECIPE-LOG.md` (documentation, not a constraint); repeating a prior client's choice is fine when it converts.

### Hero archetypes

1. Full-bleed photo or video, centered stack
2. Split 50/50 — copy one side, image the other
3. Asymmetric offset — oversized type overlapping image
4. Editorial masthead — huge type, rule, image band below
5. Collage / card-stack, layered
6. Color block — no photo; type, texture, one graphic device
7. Diagonal or clipped-path split
8. Muted video loop with scrim
9. Before/after slider as the hero
10. Map-anchored — service area as the opening statement
11. Qualifier-first — "What do you need help with?" as the first interaction
12. Split with live proof — copy plus rotating real review or recent-job feed

### Services layouts

1. Icon card grid *(the cliché — requires justification)*
2. Alternating full-width rows with imagery
3. Numbered editorial list *(only if genuinely sequential)*
4. Horizontal-scroll tall cards
5. Tabbed panels
6. Bento grid, mixed cell sizes
7. Accordion with inline imagery
8. Package / tier cards
9. Sticky-scroll: pinned image column, scrolling copy
10. Index list — type-only, hover reveals image

### Nav treatments

1. Classic sticky bar
2. Two-tier: utility strip above, main nav below
3. Transparent over hero, solid on scroll
4. Centered logo, split nav
5. Minimal bar with full-screen overlay menu
6. Left logo, center nav, right CTA pill
7. Sidebar nav *(desktop only, portfolio-heavy GCs)*

### Section rhythm

Hard color-block alternation · angled or curved dividers · cards breaking section boundaries · full-bleed image bands as breathers · sticky-scroll pinned sections · rule-and-label editorial separation · type overlapping a section seam

## Typography — the biggest lever

Type does more for perceived uniqueness than color. Two sites with identical palettes and different type read as different studios. Two sites with identical type and different palettes read as the same template.

| Direction | Pairing logic |
|---|---|
| Chic / upscale salon | High-contrast display serif + neutral grotesque |
| Rugged trades | Condensed grotesque (uppercase, tight) + humanist sans |
| Technical / performance | Geometric sans + mono for numerics and specs |
| Heritage / family-owned | Slab serif + classic sans |
| Playful / pet | Rounded sans, two weights, generous letterspacing |
| Premium minimal | One neo-grotesque, tight tracking, extreme scale contrast |
| Editorial / architectural | Transitional serif + condensed sans for labels |
| Urban / streetwear | Heavy display grotesque + tight-tracked sans |

Never more than two families plus one utility face. Script faces appear on at most one word, or not at all. Set a real type scale — a display face at body size is a wasted display face.

## Banned defaults

If a direction contains one, revise it.

**The three AI-design clichés.** These appear regardless of subject and read as machine output:

1. Warm cream background (~`#F4F1EA`) + high-contrast serif + terracotta or clay accent (~`#D97757`)
2. Near-black background with a single acid-green or vermilion accent
3. Broadsheet layout — hairline rules, zero border-radius, dense newspaper columns

Legitimate when a brief *asks* for them. Never as a free choice.

**Also banned unless specifically justified:**

- Purple-to-blue gradient anything
- Three rounded cards in a row with thin outline icons above centered text
- Glassmorphism / frosted blur panels
- The generic hero: centered headline, centered subhead, two buttons, faded stock photo
- Stock photography of strangers — smiling model in a hard hat, headset support woman, generic handshake
- Big-number-plus-small-label stat rows with a gradient accent
- Blob shapes, abstract 3D gradient meshes
- `01 / 02 / 03` markers on content that isn't a sequence
- Animated counters ticking up on scroll
- Carousels for primary content
- "Trusted by thousands" without a verifiable number
- Every section fading in on scroll

---

## Restraint

Spend boldness in one place. Let the signature element be the one memorable thing; keep everything around it quiet and disciplined. Cut any decoration that doesn't serve the brief.

Not taking a risk is itself a risk — a site nobody remembers doesn't convert either. But take one, not five.

Before shipping a direction: look at it and remove one thing.
