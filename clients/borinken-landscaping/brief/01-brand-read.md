# Brand Read — Borinken Landscaping

## Who they actually are

A one-truck, hands-on operation serving Brevard County, FL — Hiram on the
tools, not in an office. The photo library backs this up literally: across
every jobsite photo, there is exactly one crew member doing the work,
always mid-task, never posed. This isn't a company that dispatches crews;
it's an owner-operator who shows up and does the job himself, with a
properly wrapped trailer and a branded uniform that says he takes the
business seriously even though it's still small. Nothing in the intake
claims years-in-business, licensing, or a fleet — this reads as an
established-but-still-personal outfit, not a franchise and not a side
hustle. The hero photo tells the real story better than any tagline could:
he's not just mowing the lawn, he's up on a pole trimmer shaping a topiary
into a clean round ball next to the flagpole — work nobody would notice if
skipped. That's a business that does the extra 10% without being asked.

## Five adjectives

Hands-on. Warm. Meticulous. Humble. Faith-rooted.

## Five adjectives they are NOT

Corporate. Luxury. Discount. Impersonal. Slick.

This rules out the two failure modes home-services sites usually fall
into: the generic "quality you can trust" franchise template, and the
overcorrected boutique-luxury look that would feel dishonest for a
one-truck operation. This is neither the cheapest guy in town nor a
premium concierge brand — it's a trustworthy, present, faith-forward small
business that happens to do beautiful, careful work.

## The customer, and their emotional state on arrival

Nobody's calling Borinken because water is coming through their ceiling.
The service area skews upscale suburban/HOA (paver driveways, tile roofs,
lakefront lots, per the asset audit) — the emotional driver here is
recurring low-grade pressure, not emergency panic: keeping the yard from
looking neglected next to the neighbors', staying ahead of an HOA
compliance letter, or simply not having the time/energy to do it
themselves every week. The anxiety is "will this person actually show up
consistently and do it right," not "help me now." That means the homepage
needs to build trust and reliability fast — real work, real person,
real follow-through — rather than manufacture urgency that isn't there.

## The one job of the homepage

Get a free estimate request started — phone call, text, or form. There's
no booking calendar, no service tiers to compare, no pricing to browse.
One job: convince a hesitant homeowner this is a real, reliable person
worth calling, then make calling or texting effortless.

## Vernacular

Mowing, weed eating, edging, planting, hedge trim, mulch beds, topiary
shaping, curb appeal, recurring lawn service, HOA-ready, the rig
(truck + trailer), "let us be your blessing." Plainspoken, listy, not
polished — his own Facebook caption is literally
"LANDSCAPINGS NEEDS, MOWING, WEED EATING, PLANTING, EDGING, AND MORE," all
caps, no fuss. That directness is a voice asset, not a flaw to smooth over.

## Voice

Two registers, both real and both his. The work itself is described
plainly and directly — a flat list of services, no embellishment, said
the way a guy who's actually out there mowing would say it. But the
business's self-description turns warm and relational fast: "Permítanos
ser su bendición — Let us be your blessing," offered bilingually, unprompted,
on his own page. That's not marketing copy, that's how he actually thinks
about the work — a service rendered with some humility and care attached,
not just a transaction. The copy should sound like a person who's
straightforward about what he does and sincere about why he does it, never
salesy, never over-adjectived. Short sentences. Real words. No "we pride
ourselves on quality craftsmanship."

## Competitive read

Local lawn/landscaping sites in Florida are almost uniformly templated:
stock photography of impossibly perfect lawns, a generic green-and-white
palette, "quality service you can trust" boilerplate, and heavy
storm/hurricane-cleanup upsell copy that has nothing to do with the actual
day-to-day service. None of them show a real person doing real work, and
none of them have a distinctive brand mark — most competitors run a
generic grass-blade or shovel icon. Borinken already has something no
competitor can copy: a real, personal coquí/Taíno mark tied to the owner's
Puerto Rico roots, a real photo of him working, and a genuinely warm,
bilingual voice already in his own words. The opening is authenticity
over polish — lean into the real photo, the real mark, and the real
sentence he already wrote about being a blessing to his customers, instead
of sanding it down into another templated lawn-care site.
