A mower gets the middle of the yard. It doesn't get the strip along your fence line, the grass creeping over the edge of a paver walkway, or the overgrowth tight around a mailbox post or sprinkler head. That's where weed eating comes in — it's the difference between a yard that's "mowed" and a yard that's actually finished.

Left alone, that grass grows shaggy and uneven around every edge, and it's usually the first thing a neighbor or an HOA notices, even when the rest of the lawn looks fine. Hiram works the trimmer along fences, beds, driveways, and anywhere the mower deck can't sit flush, so the whole property reads clean, not just the open grass.

It's typically done as part of a regular mowing visit, so the two jobs finish looking like one job — no rough edges left behind to deal with later.
