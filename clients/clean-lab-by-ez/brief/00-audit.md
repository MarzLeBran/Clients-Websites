# Asset Audit — Clean Lab by EZ

## Photo grade: C
13 files in `photos/`, one of which (`image.jpg`) is not a raw work photo at all — it's a
finished marketing graphic (dark swirl background, baked-in headline text, logo lockup
already composited on top of a stock-feeling VW shot). That leaves 12 real photos. Both
numbers sit under the 20-photo floor for a B grade.

The count is the binding problem, not the skill behind the camera. Seven of the twelve are
genuinely good exterior "after" shots — sharp focus, real daylight, varied makes (Cadillac
Escalade IQ, Chevrolet Corvette C8, two Lexus, two Tesla, one Ford F-150) shot in a
consistent front-or-3/4-front driveway style. If there were 40 of these, this would be a B.
There are 7.

The other five drag the average down: `headlight-restoration.png` is shot on a folding
table with unrelated vehicles cluttering the background and no paired before/after, so it
can't prove the transformation it's named for; `sedan-interior-detail.jpg` shows a steering
wheel still wrapped in protective plastic (mid-service, not a finished result) plus a
promotional flyer propped on the seat; `interior-deep-cleaning.jpg` also has that same
promotional flyer-mat in frame and a real-estate flag visible through the windshield. No
before/after pairs exist anywhere in the set. No engine-bay or pet-hair-removal photo
exists despite both being sold as services. Exactly one photo has a person in it, and their
face isn't visible.

## Design implication
Type-driven. Twelve usable photos (seven strong, five compromised) can't populate a home
page, an about page, a reviews page, a services index, individual service pages, and six
city service-area pages without heavy repetition — the honest move is to lean on the
orange/black/white brand system (already proven out on the flyers and logo) as the primary
design language, use the 7 strong exterior shots as hero/accent images and social proof,
and treat everything else — icons, color blocks, bold type, the pricing table already laid
out on the flyers — as the workhorse. This also sidesteps the two structurally weak photos
(headlight, sedan-interior) rather than forcing them into slots they can't fill.

## Real-world brand evidence
No vehicle wrap, no shop signage, no storefront, no business card was found — expected,
since the business is confirmed 100% mobile with no fixed location. The brand evidence that
does exist is unusually consistent across three independent sources, which is worth noting
explicitly because it *agrees with* rather than contradicts the intake's tentative color
read:

- **Logo** (`logo/logo.png`): black background, white line-art flask/car mark, vivid orange
  accent (~#F47920-range orange), white "CLEAN LAB" / orange "by EZ" script wordmark.
- **Flyer 1** (`existing/flyer-1.PNG`) and **Flyer 2** (`existing/flyer-2.jpg`): same black
  background, same orange, white body text, orange section banners, hexagon-pattern texture
  accent. These read as professionally laid-out designs, not amateur flyers.
- **Uniform** (`assets/photos/seat-extraction-action.jpg`): a technician is shown from
  behind wearing a black hoodie with the Clean Lab by EZ logo in orange/white on the chest,
  and a crew member's first name in orange script down the sleeve ("Mathew," with a Bible
  verse reference "6:33" beneath it) — a second partial name ("...equiel," likely "Ezequiel")
  is visible on a second person's shoulder just out of frame.

Three-for-three agreement on black + vivid orange + white. This is a stronger, more
confirmed signal than "hex codes on a form" — treat it as the locked palette, not a
proposal.

## People inventory
One photo out of thirteen shows a person, and it's a back-of-the-body shot — gloved hands,
branded hoodie, no face (`seat-extraction-action.jpg`). No owner photo, no crew headshots,
no customer-facing photos exist anywhere in the asset set. For a home-services site, faces
are normally the single highest-converting element on the page, and this library has zero.
Recommend a shoot: even a phone-shot sequence of the owner/crew mid-job (faces visible),
one arrival/greeting shot, and a couple of posed-but-natural portraits would meaningfully
change what's possible here.

## Usable / unusable

**Strong — can carry a hero or lead gallery slot:**
- `black-vehicle-detailing.jpg` — black Cadillac Escalade IQ, straight-on front, high
  gloss, best single hero candidate.
- `full-detail.jpg` — same vehicle, 3/4 angle, different property (solar-panel roof
  visible in background) — good hero alternate or gallery pair with the above.
- `maintenance-detailing.png` — orange Chevrolet Corvette C8, front 3/4, color pop that
  reads well against the brand's black/orange palette.
- `suv-exterior-detail.jpeg` — silver Lexus NX, 3/4 front, clean daylight, no clutter.
- `tesla-black-gloss.png` — black Tesla, rear 3/4 at dusk, moody/premium light.
- `tesla-front-detail.jpeg` — companion close-up of the same Tesla, good detail/texture
  shot for a "gloss finish" callout.
- `truck-exterior-detail.jpg` — gray Ford F-150, 3/4 front, proves they handle full-size
  trucks (the top price tier on the flyer).
- `seat-extraction-action.jpg` — the one action/uniform shot; use it for brand-proof
  (about page, service page) despite the missing face — it's the only "real work happening"
  image in the set.

**Usable but needs cropping or reframing:**
- `suv-interior-detail.jpg` — clean Lexus rear seats, unremarkable but not compromised;
  fine as filler.
- `interior-deep-cleaning.jpg` — usable if tightly cropped to dash/seat only; as shot, a
  promotional "Hop In, Your Clean Ride Awaits" flyer mat sits on the seat and real-estate
  yard-sign flags are visible through the windshield.
- `sedan-interior-detail.jpg` — same promotional flyer-mat prop in frame, and the steering
  wheel is still wrapped in protective plastic, so it reads as an in-progress shot rather
  than a finished result. Could work as a "we protect your interior" process photo but
  should not be used as a "finished" gallery image.

**Not usable as-is:**
- `headlight-restoration.png` — shot on a folding table outdoors with unrelated parked
  vehicles cluttering the background; no context tying it to a customer's vehicle; no
  before/after pairing, so it doesn't actually demonstrate the restoration. Needs a
  proper before/after pair before it's worth using.
- `image.jpg` (the ambiguous filename) — not a raw work photo. It's a pre-built marketing
  graphic with baked-in headline text ("Mobile Car Detailing"), a composited stock-feeling
  vehicle shot, and the logo already locked up on top. It can't be re-cropped or re-treated
  for site use; file it with the flyers as existing marketing collateral, not as photo-library
  stock.

No files in the set are watermarked by a third party, and no competitor branding appears in
frame (vehicle-brand badges — VW, Honda, Cadillac, Lexus, Ford, Tesla, Chevrolet — are the
customers' own cars and are expected/harmless in a detailing portfolio).

One composition note across the usable set: nearly every shot is vertical/portrait
phone-camera orientation. That's good for a mobile-first hero but means there is currently
no true wide horizontal shot for a desktop hero band — expect to crop-and-pad rather than
find a ready-made wide image.

## Missing info — blocking
Per the intake (`intake/intake-form.md`), the following are still PENDING OPERATOR and
block parts of the Stage 3 build; flagging the ones an asset-only pass can't resolve:
- Licence number/issuing state (flag: may be N/A for FL mobile detailing — confirm, don't
  assume).
- Full weekly hours (only a single-day "Closes 5PM" snapshot exists via the GBP screenshot).
- Booking platform/scheduler — flyer shows WhatsApp/call only, no scheduler evidence in any
  asset reviewed.
- Legal business name, domain, form-submission email, street address (or explicit
  confirmation there is none to disclose).
- Photo/name consent for the crew member(s) shown and named in
  `seat-extraction-action.jpg` ("Mathew," partial "...equiel") — their name and likeness are
  already in the asset set; need consent before using on the live site.
- Whether the flyer/logo artwork has an editable source file — only raster PNG/JPG exists
  for `logo.png` and both flyers; no `.ai/.eps/.svg` was provided, so the locked color
  palette above will need to be re-derived from pixels rather than a true brand file.

## Missing info — nice to have
- A proper before/after pair for at least one signature service (headlight restoration is
  the obvious candidate — it's the one photo in the set that needs it most).
- Photos or a short shoot covering engine-bay cleaning and pet-hair removal — both are sold
  services with zero current photo evidence.
- Owner/crew face photos — see People Inventory above; this is the single highest-leverage
  gap in the whole library.
- A true wide/horizontal shot of any finished vehicle for a desktop hero band, to avoid
  cropping a portrait shot down.

---

**Highest-leverage next step:** a one-day phone shoot (no professional camera needed) that
captures three things this library is completely missing — (1) the owner's or a crew
member's face, on camera, mid-job or in a natural portrait; (2) one true before/after pair
on a real job, shot in the same framing before and after; and (3) 15-20 more exterior
"after" shots in the same driveway-front-3/4 style that's already working well for the 7
strong photos on hand. That single shoot would very likely move this from a C to a solid B
and unlock a materially more photo-forward design.
