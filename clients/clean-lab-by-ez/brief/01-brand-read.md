# Brand Read — Clean Lab by EZ

## Who they actually are

A lean, owner-operated mobile auto detailing business covering six Central Florida cities
(Kissimmee, Davenport, St. Cloud, Orlando, Poinciana, Haines City) from a base near Davenport,
FL, with no shop or storefront — they bring their own water and electricity to the customer's
home or workplace. Asset evidence points to a crew of at least two (a uniform photo shows one
person plus a partial second name), not a solo operator, but this reads small — a truck or two,
not a fleet. They've already built real marketing infrastructure well beyond what a brand-new
operation typically has: a custom logo with a considered "lab" conceit (flask/test-tube mark),
two professionally laid-out flyers (one bilingual English/Spanish), a tiered pricing menu with
a "Most Popular" tier already identified, and a live Google Business Profile carrying 25
reviews at a 5.0 average. That's a business with real traction and a few years of operating
discipline behind it, not a weekend side hustle — even though the exact founding date is still
unknown. The operator's own stated reason for starting the business — wanting to "provide the
best service possible" and "dictate my quality" — points to someone who got into this because
they wanted direct control over the work, which tracks with an owner who's still on the tools,
not managing from an office.

## Five adjectives

Precision-minded, performance-driven, mobile-first, locally-rooted, direct.

## Five adjectives they are NOT

Budget/bargain (pricing sits mid-to-premium for the category, not the cheapest option in
market), corporate/franchise (single WhatsApp-first phone number, no fleet branding or
call-center feel), fussy or exclusive (the tier structure starts at an accessible $79 refresh,
not a velvet-rope premium-only offering), sterile despite the lab conceit (bilingual, warm,
directly-reachable — not a cold clinical brand), slow or laid-back (the visual and verbal
language — "performance," "dark," Lab Reset/Refresh — is charged and urgent, not a leisurely
spa-day tone).

## The customer, and their emotional state on arrival

Someone who owns a vehicle worth caring about — the photo evidence (a Tesla, a Corvette C8, a
Cadillac Escalade IQ, two Lexus, an F-150) skews toward newer or higher-value cars — arriving in
one of two states: routine maintenance mode (wants the car looking sharp on a recurring basis
and values never having to drive anywhere to get it) or a "let it go too long" reset mode
(visible stains, odor, grime, wants a real turnaround). Neither is a plumbing-emergency level of
urgency, but both are inconvenience-averse — the entire pitch is "we come to you," so this
customer's dominant emotion is impatience with friction, not distress. They're comparing tiers
and prices, not panicking.

## The one job of the homepage

Get a name and a phone number into the pipeline — by whichever path fits the visitor. The
operator's own answer was explicit: "book an appointment or ask for more information." That's a
dual-intent CTA, not a single forced action: ready buyers should be able to book directly once
the GHL calendar is wired up; everyone else needs a low-friction way to ask a question or
request a quote without being funneled through a scheduler they're not ready for.

## Vernacular

Real service language already established on the flyers, not generic detailing-package copy:
foam cannon pre-wash, hand wash, wheel & tire cleaning, tire shine, interior vacuum,
dashboard/console/panel wipe-down, interior glass, seat/carpet/mat shampoo, stain extraction,
odor elimination, engine bay cleaning, pet hair removal, wax application. Branding vocabulary:
Lab Refresh, Lab Reset, "Lab grade clean for your car," "100% mobile service," "we bring water
and electricity to you." Build headlines and service names from these actual terms.

## Voice

Directly quoted from the operator's own answers (not the flyer copy, which reads more polished
and may not be their natural register): *"we're going to be using GHL calendar. It may change,
but for now that's what we're going to do"* — plain, practical, comfortable saying something is
provisional rather than overselling certainty. *"performance, dark, be able to provide the best
service possible, and dictate my quality"* — direct, unpolished, quality-and-control focused,
not marketing-speak. Worth flagging explicitly: the flyer taglines ("Lab grade clean for your
car," "We make it EZ," "Clean, Convenient, Professional") are noticeably more polished than how
the operator actually talks — they may have had help with the flyers, or simply write
differently than they speak. Site copy should default to the plainer, more direct register from
the operator's actual words, not just recycle flyer taglines uncritically.

## Competitive read

Mobile detailing marketing in most markets, including Central Florida, defaults to stock
photography of soap suds and microfiber cloths, pastel or corporate-blue palettes, vague
"eco-friendly" claims, and undifferentiated pricing pages that all look like the same template
with a different logo. Clean Lab by EZ already has three genuine, real (not proposed) points of
difference to build on: the "lab" framing itself — precision/scientific rather than "wash" or
"shine," which is unusual in this category; real photo evidence of higher-end vehicles most
competitors can't show; and an already-locked dark/vivid-orange palette instead of the
industry-standard blue-and-white car-wash imagery. The opening is to commit fully to "a
precision performance lab for your car," not soften it into another generic mobile-detailing
site — that's the one thing this brand has that a swap-test would catch a competitor missing.
